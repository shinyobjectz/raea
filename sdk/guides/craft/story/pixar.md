# The story spine (and how Pixar uses it)

The spine is Kenn Adams' improv exercise (1991): *Once upon a time... Every day... Until one day... Because of that...
Because of that... Until finally... And ever since then...* Emma Coats passed it on in her rules of storytelling from
her time at Pixar (2011), and it's become the shorthand for Pixar's way of breaking a story. Ed Catmull's *Creativity,
Inc.* (2014) describes the other half, the Braintrust: candid notes that name a problem and leave the fix to the
director.

In our words: the routine first, so the change reads; then a chain where each step happens *because* of the last; then
the new normal. The test is the chain. If you can say "and then" between two beats, the spine is broken there.

## The lens (`pixar`)

Stages: `once`, `every-day` (routine events), `one-day` (the inciting event), `because` (linked beats between),
`finally` (the climax), `ever-since` (after).

It finds: no routine (warn; proposes an ordinary day), nothing breaking it (error), fewer than two "because of that"
(warn), a beat after the change that isn't a consequence (error), no resolution (error), no new normal (note).

raea's version of the Braintrust is the readers and the person's notes: say the problem, not the fix.
