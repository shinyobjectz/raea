# Refine: colorize

Colour does three jobs in a video: it marks what matters, it makes words legible over moving pictures, and it makes
the piece recognisably this brand's. Work in roles, never in a bag of swatches. Keep BRAND.md's commitments; a new
palette is a direction change, not a colorize pass.

## Audit
- Which colours are the brand's commitments (BRAND.md), and which did the piece add?
- What is each colour doing: ground, ink, accent, support, the ask? A colour with no job is decoration.
- Where does the accent appear? If it's on most beats, it no longer marks anything.
- Where does text fail contrast against the actual footage behind it (bright walls, skin, moving backgrounds)?
- Does the footage's own colour fight the palette (a green wall under a red accent)?

## Choose a strategy
Name the temperature, the dominant relationship and how much colour the frame carries: restrained, committed, full
or drenched (`stages/direct`). Then map roles:
- **ground**: fields that own regions (cards, slides, end frames);
- **ink**: text, with what keeps it legible over pixels;
- **accent**: the payoff word, the proof, the ask; rare, so it has force;
- **support**: labels, secondary lines, dividers;
- **grade**: how the footage itself is nudged toward the palette, if at all (gently; skin stays skin).

## Apply
- Let the strongest colour own a role completely instead of scattering small touches.
- On a coloured ground, secondary text is a tint or shade of that ground or the ink, never a flat grey.
- Contrast is measured against what's really behind the glyphs: 3:1 at least for large type over footage. When a
  brand colour can't make it, add a plate or scrim role rather than abandoning the colour.
- Keep a colour's meaning stable across the piece: if the accent means "the proof", it means that every time.

## Verify
Every colour has a job; the eye lands where the beat wants it; text reads over every frame it sits on; the frame is
recognisably this brand with the logo covered.
