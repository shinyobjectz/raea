# Refine: distill

Strip the piece to what it's for. Every line, part and sound earns its place or leaves. Expect to cut good things
to make room for the one great one.

## Find the essence
- What is the one thing the viewer should keep? There is one.
- Which lines carry it, and which restate it, set it up twice, or carry a second idea?
- Which parts repeat what is already said or shown?

## Cut
- **Whole lines, never words inside them.** Each line is a cut from a take; trimming words makes lines no take
  says. Drop the second idea entirely rather than squeezing both.
- **On-screen copy, then again.** Halve the words on screen, then halve again. A caption group of one to three
  words; a card with one statement, not three.
- **Parts.** One part per idea at most. A beat the person carries alone needs no graphic.
- **Variation.** Fewer colours, fewer faces, fewer kinds of motion. One way to mark emphasis.
- **Sound.** One bed or none, one foley family, used less.

Don't cut what the viewer needs to believe the claim (the proof stays), don't remove the ask, don't make it so spare
it's unclear: mystery is not minimalism.

## Verify
Watch once: can you say the one thing in a sentence? Is anything left that you'd cut next? Is everything needed to
believe it still there?
