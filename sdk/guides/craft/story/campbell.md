# Campbell: the monomyth

Joseph Campbell, *The Hero with a Thousand Faces* (1949). From myths across cultures he drew one pattern in three
movements, departure, initiation and return, with stages inside each. In our words: someone is called out of the
known world, may refuse, gets help, crosses into the unknown, is tested on a road of trials down to an ordeal at the
bottom, takes what they came for (the boon), and brings it back to a world that needs it.

raea reads the monomyth as a curve: the ordeal is the lowest point before the boon, and the boon must lift the story
above it. Most of Campbell's seventeen stages don't fit a 30 second film and shouldn't be forced; the curve does.

## The lens (`campbell`)

Stages: `call`, `refusal` (beats after the call that turn down), `aid` (where a mentor or gift first acts), `threshold`
(the hero's first linked act after the call and the aid), `trials`, `ordeal`, `boon`, `return`.

It finds: no call (error), no mentor or gift (warn), no descent after the threshold (warn), a boon that doesn't lift
the story above the ordeal (error: the hero brings nothing back), no return (note).

Reach for it on journeys: a quest, a move, a first time. For the roles characters play along the way, read
[vogler](vogler.md).
