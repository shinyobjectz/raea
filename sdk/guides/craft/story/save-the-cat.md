# Save the Cat: the beat sheet

Blake Snyder, *Save the Cat! The Last Book on Screenwriting You'll Ever Need* (2005). Fifteen beats placed by page
number in a 110-page script. In our words: an opening image; the theme said aloud by someone; the set-up; a catalyst;
a debate; a break into a new world; a B story; fun and games (the premise's promise); a midpoint where the stakes rise
(a false victory or a false defeat); the bad guys closing in; all is lost; a dark night; a break into the finale; the
finale; and a final image that mirrors the first, changed. The title names a moment early on where the hero does
something that makes us like them.

raea places the beats by share of the telling, not by page, so a 30 second ad and a feature are held to the same
shape. It can't see likeability or theme spoken aloud, and doesn't pretend to.

## The lens (`save-the-cat`)

Stages: `opening-image`, `catalyst`, `debate`, `break-into-two`, `midpoint`, `bad-guys-close-in`, `all-is-lost`,
`finale`, `final-image`.

It finds: a catalyst past the first third (warn), no midpoint turn in a telling of five beats or more (note), the
lowest point in the first half (warn), and a final image at the same level as the opening one (warn).

Reach for it on trailers and longer films where pacing by position matters.
