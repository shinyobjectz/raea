# Kishōtenketsu: a turn, not a fight

The four-part form of classical Chinese poetry (qǐ chéng zhuǎn hé), carried into Japanese as ki-shō-ten-ketsu and into
the four-panel yonkoma comic. In our words: **ki** introduces, **shō** develops, **ten** turns (something new arrives
that makes what came before read differently), **ketsu** brings them together.

It needs no conflict. Nothing has to oppose anyone; the interest is in the turn of perspective. That makes it right
for a lot of what Western frameworks call broken: a calm brand film, a reveal, a piece about noticing.

## The lens (`kishotenketsu`)

Stages: `ki`, `sho`, `ten` (the first beat in the second half that reveals a fact, turns hard, or is marked `surprise:
"meant"`), `ketsu`.

It never asks for a crisis or a villain. It finds: no turn at all (error), a turn in the first half before there's
anything for it to change (warn), and a story that stops on the turn with no ketsu (warn).

Reach for it on quiet pieces and reveals, and to check a "nothing happens" note: often something does, late.
