# Mode: direct-response

An ad that asks for something: a tap, a purchase, a sign-up, a download. Paid placement, a feed that doesn't know
the brand, a viewer who owes it nothing.

## The viewer succeeds when
They stop in the first second, believe one claim by the time it's proven, and know exactly what to do and why now.
One claim, believed, acted on. A viewer who enjoyed it but can't say what it offers was entertained, not persuaded.

## What ambition looks like
- **A first frame only this product could make.** Not a greeting or a logo: the product doing the surprising
  thing, the result before the setup, the line that names the viewer's problem in their words. Test it: would the
  same opening work for a competitor? Then it isn't the hook yet.
- **Proof on screen as it's said.** The claim and its evidence arrive together: the product working, the real
  number with its source, the before and after, the creator's actual use. Proof is the design's main job here.
- **The ask in working form.** The offer itself (the code, the price as BRAND.md states it, the handle, the
  action) inside the zones and held long enough to act on, not a vague "link in bio" floating at the end.
- **The world can lead.** Persuasion is where the direction may commit fully: palette, type and a signature move
  that the viewer would recognise with the words removed. Conversion lives inside the direction's own vocabulary;
  a committed look that hides the offer hasn't finished translating.

## The rut to name
The category's ad: problem-agitate-solution read to camera, white captions with a highlighted word, a stat card,
"link in bio". And its predictable opposite (the deliberately lo-fi anti-ad). Both are in the rut; strategy picks
from outside it or plays one of them straight on purpose.

## Watch for
- Claims: every one in BRAND.md's allowed claims or said in the footage. An ad is where the pressure to invent is
  highest; resist it.
- Platform policy: before/after and health or money claims carry rules. When in doubt, show less and say so.
- Shape: 4:5 and 1:1 feed placements often run muted and small; lead with the picture and the claim, not the talk.
  9:16 lives on the first second and the words on screen.

Rubric weight: all ten heuristics apply; 1, 3 and 4 decide whether it works.
