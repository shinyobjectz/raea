# Mode: organic

A creator's own post: a story, an opinion, a lesson, a day, a reaction. It runs in the feed of people who chose to
follow, or who might. There may be a soft ask (follow, part two, comment); there is no hard sell.

## The viewer succeeds when
They watch to the end, feel they know the person a little better, and want the next one. Completion and return are
the measure. A viewer who notices the editing more than the person was served the edit, not the creator.

## What ambition looks like
- **The person leads.** Their face, voice and timing are the piece. Design frames them: it never competes with them
  for the eye. Cut for their best reads and keep the moments that sound like them, imperfections included when they
  carry character.
- **Captions carry the voice.** Breaks where they breathe, emphasis where they lean, a treatment that fits how they
  talk (dry, fast, warm, deadpan). Captions here are the main design surface; give them the most thought.
- **One signature move.** A single recurring device the audience can come to recognise (a way of marking the key
  word, a sound on the turn, a framing for asides), used where it means something, not on every line.
- **Restraint and pacing.** Fewer parts than an ad, each earning its place. A beat of quiet lands harder than a
  card. The world lends type, palette and the signature move; it doesn't wrap the person in a costume.

## The rut to name
Over-produced: a card on every line, a zoom on every sentence, music wall to wall, the creator reduced to a
background for graphics. Its opposite is under-produced: raw talk with default captions and nothing decided. Both
lose the person.

## Watch for
- Authenticity: don't put words in their mouth (cut only what's said) and don't polish away what makes them
  recognisable.
- The ending: an organic piece ends on a line or a look, not a fade into an end card, unless the creator's own
  posts do that.
- Series: when the object or brand has earlier pieces, keep the creator's recognisable treatment and vary the angle.

Rubric weight: 3 (proof) is often n/a; 1, 5, 7 and 9 decide it. 4 (the close) is a payoff or a loop more often
than an ask.
