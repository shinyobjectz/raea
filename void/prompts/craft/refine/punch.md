# Refine: punch (bolder)

"Bolder", "more punch", "it's flat" is almost always about one beat that already exists. Raise that beat to the
conviction the rest of the piece already implies, without rebuilding anything else. The reflex answer, more
effects, is the opposite of bold; reject it first.

## Scope is sovereign
Touch only the named beat (or the one the critique named). Don't restyle its neighbours, don't add colours, faces
or kinds of motion the piece doesn't already own. If the direction genuinely can't express what's needed, say
what one addition would do and ask.

## Why it reads flat
A flat beat usually opts out of the piece's own strongest moves. Look at what the strongest beat does that this one
doesn't: the display face at full size, the accent on the word that matters, a punch-in on the line, a hit on the
downbeat, a cut that lands on the word, a held silence before it.

## The move
- **Use what the piece owns, at full strength.** Its display face bigger, its accent on this beat's payoff word,
  its signature move here. The bolder beat looks more like the same piece, not less.
- **Commit, then clear.** Make the one decisive move completely, then quiet everything around it (fewer words on
  screen, the bed dipped, the neighbours' motion calmer) so the move reads. If everything got louder, nothing did.
- **Give it its own rhythm.** A peak is a change: a shorter shot after long ones, a hold after fast cuts, silence
  after sound.
- **Keep it true.** The beat's claim and words stay as they are unless the person supplies new ones.

## The skeleton test
Mute it and blank every word. Does the beat's hierarchy still read: is it obvious this is the moment that matters,
from scale, timing, framing and motion alone? If it only works once the words return, the boldness is in the type
size, not the design.

## Before you finish
Everything outside the beat is unchanged; nothing new entered the system; the beat is unmistakably the same piece,
only surer of itself.
