# PAS: problem, agitate, solve

A direct-response copywriting formula, decades old and with no single author: name the problem the viewer has, make
it hurt (agitate: what it costs, how it gets worse), then show the fix, then the proof. It's the shape of most good 15
and 30 second ads, because it's the shortest path from recognition to relief.

In our words: the problem must be the viewer's own (they recognise it), the agitation must cost something visible,
the solve must come from the product, and the proof must be shown.

## The lens (`pas`)

Stages: `problem` (a beat that turns the focus value down), `agitate` (lower still, or the cost made plain), `solve`
(the turn up, with the product), `proof` (a claim backed by a fact the story makes true).

It finds: no problem (error), a problem that arrives late for an ad (warn), no agitation (note), no solve (error), a
solve that doesn't show the product (warn), no proof (note).

Reach for it on short ads. On anything longer than 45 seconds it reads as a pitch; use `storynomics` and `mckee`.
