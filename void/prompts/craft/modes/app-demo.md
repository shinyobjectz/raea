# Mode: app-demo

A piece built on the real product's screens: a feature launch, a walkthrough, a "watch me do X in 20 seconds", an
app store preview.

## The viewer succeeds when
They see the job done in the real product and believe they could do it themselves. The product's own screens are
the proof; the edit's job is to make them legible on a phone.

## What ambition looks like
- **The real screens are the hero.** Recorded UI, never a mock-up drawn to look like it. When the recording is
  small or busy, frame it: crop to the region that matters, punch in on the change, let the rest go.
- **Zooms land on the change.** Every move of the camera goes to where something is about to happen (the button
  pressed, the result appearing) and arrives just before it does. A zoom that hunts is worse than none.
- **Time compressed honestly.** Cut the waits, speed the boring stretches, keep the moment of result at real speed
  so it reads as real. Never fake a speed the product doesn't have.
- **The world lends type, palette and one move.** The product's UI already has its own look; the edit's direction
  frames it (callouts, captions, the open and close) without fighting it. Callout style takes its cue from BRAND.md.

## The rut to name
A screen recording at full size with a cursor wandering; device mock-ups floating at an angle on a gradient;
feature-list cards between clips.

## Watch for
- Legibility: UI text in a recording is rarely readable on a phone at full frame. Crop until the important text
  meets the floor, or caption what it says.
- Order: show the job in the order a user does it; a demo that skips the step the viewer worries about loses trust.
- Claims: speed, ease and results stay what the recording shows.

Rubric weight: 3 and 6 decide it; 1 is the result shown first or the problem named; 4 is where to get it.
