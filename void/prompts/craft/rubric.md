# Your critique rubric

Use this to judge your own piece before you call it done (`stages/critique` says when and how). Score what is on
screen and in the ear, from a render, never from what you meant to make.

## The ten heuristics
Score each 0-4: 0 absent or broken, 1 weak, 2 works but generic, 3 strong and specific, 4 nothing to add. Mark a
heuristic n/a when the mode makes it meaningless (a game has no ask; a deck has no hook frame) and leave it out of
the total.

1. **The first second.** Mute it and watch only to 1.5 s. Is there a picture, a word or a sound a thumb would stop
   for, and does it open a question the rest answers? A logo, a greeting or a slow push on a wall scores 0-1.
2. **One thing to keep.** After one watch, can you say the single idea in one sentence a viewer would use? Two
   competing ideas cap this at 2.
3. **Proof in the picture.** When the piece claims something, does the frame show it at the moment it's said (the
   product working, the result, the receipt, the source)? Telling without showing caps this at 2.
4. **The close.** Does the end land: an ask the viewer can act on, a payoff, a loop back to the open? A piece that
   just stops scores 0-1.
5. **Rhythm.** Shot lengths vary with meaning; nothing sits still longer than its content earns; energy has a
   shape (builds, breathes, lands) rather than one level. Cuts fall on words or beats.
6. **Read under the thumb.** Every word that matters is big enough, sharp against what's behind it, on screen long
   enough, and out of the platform's UI zones. Any floor defect here scores 0.
7. **Words on screen.** Captions and type carry the voice: broken at phrases, emphasis on the words that carry
   meaning, a clear hierarchy between hook, emphasis, caption and label. Default captions with no decisions score 2.
8. **Sound.** The voice is clear; music and foley support moments instead of filling space; silence is used; the
   mix is mastered. A bed at one level under everything with a whoosh per entrance scores 1-2.
9. **One world.** Palette roles, faces, caption treatment, motion and sound feel like one direction held from the
   first frame to the last, true to BRAND.md. Parts that look borrowed from different pieces cap this at 2.
10. **Specificity.** Cover the logo and the product name: could another brand post this unchanged? Could you guess
    its look from the category alone? Yes to either scores 0-1. The score rises with what only this brand, this
    person and this idea could have made.

Total = sum / (4 × heuristics scored) as a percentage. 90+ ship it. 70-89 fix the P1s and ship. 50-69 a real
pass is owed. 30-49 rethink a stage (usually strategy or direct). Below 30 start over from strategy.

## Watch as four viewers
Walk the piece once as each, and note where each one leaves or stops believing:
- **The sound-off scroller**: thumb moving, sound off, gives it one second. Do the picture and the words alone
  stop them and carry the idea?
- **The skeptic**: has seen a thousand ads like it. Where does a claim feel unearned, a cut feel like it hides
  something, a line sound scripted?
- **The person who already knows the brand**: does this feel like them, or like a template with their logo on it?
- **The platform reviewer**: anything misleading, unsupported, unreadable, or clipped by the app's UI?
Add the mode's own viewer when it has one (the learner for an explainer, the player for a game, the decision maker
for a deck).

## Severity
| | Means | Do |
|---|---|---|
| **P0** | Breaks the piece: a floor defect or ban, a claim with no evidence, the idea doesn't come through | Fix before anything else |
| **P1** | A viewer leaves or stops believing here: a weak first second, proof missing, a muddy ask | Fix in this pass |
| **P2** | Costs some viewers some attention: a slow patch, a generic part, a mix that fights the voice | Fix if the pass has room |
| **P3** | Polish: a few frames of timing, an alignment, a colour step | Fix last, or leave |

Unsure between two levels? Ask whether a viewer would scroll away at that moment. If yes, it's at least P1.

Each issue names: the moment (time or beat), what's wrong as the viewer meets it, its severity, and the playbook
that fixes it (`refine/pace`, `refine/typeset`, a return to `stages/direct` ...).
