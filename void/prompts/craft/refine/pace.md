# Refine: pace

Pacing is how time feels: where the piece rushes, where it breathes, where it drags. Fix it in the cut and the
script first; motion can't rescue a slow cut.

## Diagnose
Watch it once at speed and mark every moment your attention dips. For each, find the cause:
- **dead air**: a pause inside the talk that carries nothing (`raea check` reports it);
- **a slow line**: a line that restates, warms up, or explains what the picture already shows;
- **a flat stretch**: shots of the same length and energy back to back, nothing changing on screen;
- **a rushed beat**: a claim, a number or a turn replaced before it landed;
- **a late start**: the first thing worth stopping for arrives after 1.5 s.

## Fix
- **Hook.** Bring the strongest picture or line forward until it's in by 0.5 s. A line from the middle of a take
  often makes the better opening; move it.
- **Cut whole lines, not words inside them.** Drop the weakest lines first (restatements, warm-ups, "so
  basically"). A trimmed word is a line no take says.
- **Pauses.** Inside talk, a gap the meaning doesn't need goes. A pause that lands a turn or a punchline stays,
  on purpose; that's breathing, not dead air.
- **Vary shot length with meaning.** Short shots for momentum and lists, a longer hold for the proof and the
  payoff. Uniform lengths read as a template.
- **Cut on something.** On a word boundary, on a beat of the music, on a movement. Never mid-syllable.
- **Give landings time.** After a number, a claim or a punchline, hold long enough to read it plus a beat before
  the next thing takes the eye.
- **Shape the energy.** Decide where the piece peaks (usually the proof or the turn) and let the rest lead to it
  and away from it; one level throughout is flat at any speed.
- **Length.** When asked for a length, cut until it fits, weakest lines first, and say what went.

## Verify
`raea check` clean, the length within the ask, and a watch at speed with no dip you can't name a reason for.
