# Propp: the folktale's functions

Vladimir Propp, *Morphology of the Folktale* (1928; in English 1958). From a hundred Russian fairy tales he drew
thirty-one functions (what a character does for the plot, whoever does it) in a fixed order, and seven roles that
carry them: the villain, the donor, the helper, the sought-for person, the dispatcher, the hero and the false hero.

In our words, the spine every tale shares: a villainy or a lack; the hero leaves; a donor tests the hero and, if they
pass, gives them something; the hero struggles with the villain and wins; the lack is made good; the hero returns and
is recognised.

## The lens (`propp`)

Masks carry the roles: `shadow` is the villain, `mentor` or `gift` the donor, `herald` the dispatcher. Stages: `lack`,
`departure`, `test` (a dilemma before the gift), `gift`, `struggle` (the villain and hero in one event),
`liquidation` (the lack made good), `return`, `recognition` (a reveal after).

It finds: no lack or villainy (error), a lack never made good (error), a gift given with no test (note), and a villain
who never meets the hero (warn).

Reach for it on quests, game missions and anything told like a tale.
