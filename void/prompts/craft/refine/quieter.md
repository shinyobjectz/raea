# Refine: quieter

Quiet is harder than loud: restraint needs precision. Reduce intensity without losing the point of view or going
generic. Generic quiet is default white captions over footage with nothing decided; quiet still has a direction.

## Name the intensity sources
List what makes it loud, with where each happens:
- **caption density**: too many words on screen at once, or a highlight on every line;
- **cuts per second**: cutting faster than the meaning changes;
- **motion**: every part entering big, punch-ins on every line, bounces;
- **sound**: a bed too high or too busy, foley on every entrance;
- **colour**: saturation everywhere, the accent spent on everything;
- **parts**: a card on every beat, more graphics than ideas.
Ask what is working and what carries the piece; keep the one source that carries it.

## Reduce
- **Captions**: fewer words per group, emphasis on fewer words, a lighter weight or a smaller size within the
  floor, more time per group.
- **Cuts**: let shots run when nothing new is being said; keep cuts where the meaning turns.
- **Motion**: shorter distances, gentler ease-outs, entrances that fade or settle instead of pop; remove motion
  that doesn't serve a moment. Never bounce.
- **Sound**: bed down and simpler, foley only on the one or two moments that matter, silence where it helps.
- **Colour**: fewer colours, the accent rare again, neutrals doing more work; on colour, tint rather than grey.
- **Parts**: remove the ones that repeat what is said or shown; let the person carry beats alone.

Don't flatten the hierarchy (something still leads), don't strip all colour, don't make everything small and
light, don't remove the signature move.

## Verify
Still the same piece? Still distinctive? Easier to watch? The point of view survives the cuts.
