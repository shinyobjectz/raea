# Bernays: arranged proof, never invented

Edward Bernays, *Propaganda* (1928; public domain). He described persuasion as engineering: arrange circumstances that
make your point evident (an event people see and talk about rather than an ad that tells them), let it be carried by
the voices a group already trusts, and tie it to what people already want. The book is candid that this can be used to
mislead, and it was.

raea keeps the craft and refuses the rest. In our words: **a claim is only ever arranged proof.** You choose which true
thing to show, when, and through whom. You never show a thing that isn't true, and never say what you can't show.

## The lens (`bernays`)

Stages: `desire` (the customer's want, at the start), `circumstance` (the beat by which the audience has seen the proof),
`witness` (someone besides the brand carrying it), `claim` (where the claim lands).

It finds:
- **invented proof** (error): the claim names proof no event makes true;
- **said before seen** (warn): the claim lands before the audience knows its proof;
- **only the brand vouches** (warn): no customer or third party makes or witnesses the proof;
- **beside the point** (note): the claim doesn't meet the customer's own want.

Reach for it on every piece that makes a claim, beside `storynomics`. A brand's numbers on screen must be true in
every frame; this lens is the story-level half of that rule.
